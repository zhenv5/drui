# ASO cycle checklist and working record

Use this when preparing a full cycle. Keep records concise enough to compare the next cycle.

## Inputs to collect

- Store listing URL, app name, category, supported platforms, and actual core features.
- Target storefront and language; work on one market first.
- Target user and the need they are trying to solve.
- Current platform-specific listing fields, price, rating/review context, icon, screenshots, and preview video if present.
- A small relevant competitor set and the comparison dimension being researched.
- Current keyword positions and available demand/competition signals.
- Analytics baseline and date range; recent releases, promotions, paid campaigns, pricing, or rating changes.

If an input is missing, state the assumption and continue with a provisional plan. Do not fabricate product capabilities, search volume, rank, conversion, or download numbers. Browse current official platform documentation for limits, policies, asset specifications, and experiment availability; do not hard-code remembered platform rules as current.

## Keyword working table

| Search term | Storefront/language | User intent | Relevance evidence | Popularity | Difficulty | Position | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use the table as an evidence record, not a score formula. Popularity and Difficulty depend on the tool and should not be reported as exact App Store search counts. Mark measurements with source and observation date. Exclude terms whose result-page intent does not match the product, even if a tool shows an attractive score.

Useful discovery sources include product reviews and support requests, user interviews, relevant store listings, and search suggestions. Google Trends can reveal vocabulary and broad interest over a selected region/time window; it cannot verify App Store search volume. Any term found by an assistant should be checked in the ASO tool or other cited source before it is treated as measured.

For competitor analysis, compare only observable details: target intent, title/subtitle/description patterns, screenshot story, icon/preview, category, public rating/review themes, and localization coverage. Separate direct competitors from adjacent alternatives. A competitor's use of a term or creative choice is evidence of positioning, not proof that it causes ranking or conversion.

## Metadata review

For each candidate set, provide:

- App name and character count.
- Subtitle and character count.
- Keyword field and character count, preserving the required delimiter format for the current locale/store.
- Primary intent targeted and rationale for term placement.
- Fact check: every benefit/feature claim is supported by supplied product information.
- Policy/console check: current limits and validation are checked in App Store Connect before submission.

For Apple, distinguish discovery-oriented fields from promotional or explanatory copy, and verify live requirements in Apple's product page and App Store Connect documentation. For Google Play, use the Play-specific fields and character constraints; do not add an Apple-style keyword field.

Save the current text before publishing. Record the exact effective date after submission/approval; do not assume the save date is the effective date.

## Screenshot storyboard

| Slot | User question | Message | Real screen/evidence | Review |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What can I get? | Show the main outcome | Finished result or core screen | Clear at phone size; truthful |
| 2 | How do I start? | Show the first useful action | Real selection/start flow | Understandable without narration |
| 3 | What can I adjust? | Show a differentiating capability | Real control/edit screen | Feature exists in the current app |

Use one benefit per screenshot, short copy, consistent typography and spacing, and correct device sizes. Ask a person unfamiliar with the app what it does after seeing the first three. If they misunderstand, revise the message or evidence. Treat design preference as a hypothesis; use a controlled product-page experiment where eligible and sufficiently trafficked.

Also review the app icon and preview video when present. A description audit should examine the opening lines, clarity, feature proof, and unsupported claims. Do not assume description keyword repetition improves ranking. Use current official platform guidance for accepted media and policy rules.

## Review and reputation signals

When public reviews or user-provided feedback are available, group recurring themes by user goal, praise, friction, bug, and feature request. Note sample size, locale, rating, and date range. Treat reviews as qualitative evidence, not a representative survey unless sampling supports that claim. Feed product issues and unmet expectations into the conversion/retention diagnosis. Draft courteous, factual responses only when asked; never recommend gating, incentives, or other manipulative rating practices.

## Experiment brief

| Item | Record |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | One observable reason the proposed variant may improve the outcome |
| Variable | The main field or asset changed |
| Audience/locale | Eligible platform audience and storefront |
| Primary metric | The outcome tied to the hypothesis |
| Guardrails | Relevant quality metrics, such as activation or retention |
| Evidence plan | Current traffic, platform estimate, and expected limitation |
| Decision rule | What evidence supports adopt, reject, or continue |

Use platform-native experiments when available and verify eligibility and mechanics in current official documentation. Do not invent sample-size requirements, significance thresholds, or guaranteed timelines. If traffic is too low, call the outcome directional or inconclusive. Avoid changing multiple major variables in one test unless the design can isolate their effects.

## Measurement record

Record before and after values with the same storefront, comparable date window, and device filters where available:

- Keyword observations: source, date, Popularity, Difficulty, Position.
- Product-page exposure and conversion measures available in App Store Connect.
- App Store Search first-time downloads, with awareness that Search can include search-result advertising.
- Activation, retention, or paid conversion when relevant to app value.
- Concurrent campaigns, featuring, releases, price changes, or unusual review/ratings shifts.
- Any live experiments and their allocation/status, if available.

Use at least a complete week as an initial read; low volume needs a longer window. Avoid attributing changes to ASO when paid search or other interventions overlapped. A rank increase alone is not success. Diagnose the funnel:

- Rank improves, exposure does not: check search demand and whether the position is visible.
- Exposure/page views improve, downloads do not: check intent match, screenshots, price, and perceived value.
- Downloads improve, activation/retention does not: check whether onboarding and the product fulfill the page promise.
- No measurable movement: verify that changes became effective in the correct locale, then reconsider the terms and baseline.

Change one major element per cycle when practical, log every change, and choose the next action from the observed bottleneck. Avoid composite scores unless the user requests one and the scoring rubric and source data are explicit; otherwise prioritize actions by evidence, impact, and effort without false precision.
